# v0.3 compatibility design

v0.3 is a corrective, test-first upgrade for an already printed v0.2 body.
It deliberately leaves `minitel_body_v0.2.stl` unchanged.

## Design approach

- A separate carrier receives the four legacy v0.2 locating pins. The socket
  bores are re-cut after the connecting ribs are united, so no rib blocks a
  body pin.
- Its inner rail follows the outer route from iodeo's v2.2 Gerber rather than
  assuming a rectangle. Five 3.35 mm support bosses and 0.82 mm tapered nubs
  use the five nominal 1.143 mm PCB holes.
- Flexible hooks at the middle of the top and bottom edges retain the PCB.
  Pressing the board straight onto the nubs moves both hooks outward; they
  return over the front edge when the board is seated.
- The carrier shifts the PCB 10 mm right. The PCB back is at y=10.40 mm,
  leaving 3.40 mm between it and the rear contour rail. This reserves space
  for parts on the USB-C/reset side while keeping both controls within the
  existing side-opening depth range.
- The front is split into a shallow inset lid and a separately printed
  keyboard. Both parts can be oriented with broad, flat faces on the build
  plate and should need substantially less support than `minitel_front_v0.2`.
- A separate reset plunger has a 4.8 mm visible cap, 2.2 mm shaft and shallow
  3.35 mm retaining collar. The cap is intended to project about 1 mm beyond
  the body.

## Test order

1. Print only [`minitel_carrier_v0.3-test.stl`](stl/minitel_carrier_v0.3-test.stl).
   Rotate it so the broad, flat
   rear rail lies on the build plate; the bosses and clips point upward.
2. Slide it over the four existing v0.2 pins without forcing it.
3. Orient the board with the ESP32 module toward the open front/lid and the
   USB-C/reset component side toward the carrier. Align all five holes with
   the tapered nubs and press it in straight until both edge clips return.
4. Verify USB-C, RESET and JST routing before fitting the lid. To remove the
   PCB, move the top and bottom hooks outward gently while lifting the board.
5. Test the reset plunger manually; shorten its inner tip if necessary.
6. Only after the electronics fit, print the lid and keyboard.

## Prototype warning

This is a dimensional recovery prototype based on the v0.2 shell, the official
Gerber and owner-supplied physical-fit photographs. OpenSCAD boolean checks show
no solid overlap between the carrier and unchanged v0.2 body. The upstream
files do not provide reliable component-height solids, so the 3.40 mm rear
clearance, connector alignment and snap force still require the physical test.
Do not glue parts or remove legacy posts. Report tight sockets, clip force and
connector offsets in millimetres before printing the lid.
