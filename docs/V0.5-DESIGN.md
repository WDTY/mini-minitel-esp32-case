# v0.5 complete Minitel case

v0.5 is a complete three-part enclosure for the owner's populated iodeo ESP
Minitel V2 **JST** board. Its exterior follows the project's Minitel avatar;
no geometry from the iodeo electronics enclosure is used.

## Print files

Three current STL files are required:

1. `minitel_case_v0.5.stl` — hollow Minitel cabinet with fixed CRT front,
   vents, bridge ribs and lower rear bay opening; exported on its flat rear.
2. `minitel_keyboard_v0.5.stl` — separate flat-bottomed keyboard, normal keys,
   green Enter-key boundary, front lip and four locating tongues.
3. `minitel_esp32_bay_v0.5.stl` — removable cartridge with PCB edge guides,
   60 mm cable-turn envelope, rear USB-C opening, cable guides, releasable PCB
   latch and flexible reset plunger.

The assembled case measures approximately **78 × 123 × 74 mm**, including the
open keyboard. The bay measures **70 × 66.6 × 21 mm** at its closing flange.
Every part fits comfortably on a Creality K2 build plate.

## Assembly route

1. Push the four keyboard tongues straight into the clearance pockets in the
   lower front of the cabinet. Do not glue this first physical test.
2. Connect the JST plug to the unpowered PCB before putting it in the bay.
3. From the open inner end of the bay, feed the broad USB-C edge into the two
   flared edge guides and slide the board toward the bay's rear face.
4. Lay the cable in a relaxed U: forward from the JST, around the open front of
   the bay and back along the right side. Lay the 4.3 mm cable from above into
   the two outside guides and the open-top rear notch. The 15.5 mm DIN plug
   remains outside and never has to pass through a printed hole.
5. Check that USB-C is centred in its rear opening and that the protruding
   printed reset pad moves freely.
6. The floor cantilever clicks behind the narrow JST-side PCB nose. To remove
   the PCB, press this tab downward with a small plastic tool while sliding the
   board toward the open end.
7. Slide the complete loaded bay into the opening at the lower rear of the
   Minitel until its recessed flange meets the positive cabinet shoulder. The
   shallow side beads provide light retention.

The PCB rests only in 1.0 mm edge channels. There is no snap-on board plate.

## Creality K2 baseline

- 0.4 mm nozzle and fixed 0.20 mm layers.
- PLA for the first print.
- Print all parts in their exported orientation. The case is already rotated
  with its broad rear face on Z=0; do not stand it upright.
- 3 walls, 5 top/bottom layers and 12–15% gyroid infill.
- The hollow cabinet needs no internal infill. Three permanent 1.2 mm ribs
  reduce the final front-wall bridge to 24 mm. Set bridge direction across the
  cabinet width and inspect those layers in preview before printing.
- Keep automatic support disabled inside the hollow cabinet: it would be hard
  to remove through the bay opening. The rear bay opening starts at the plate.
- Keep support out of the PCB guide slots, reset tongue and keyboard pockets.
- Do not scale the models and leave XY/hole compensation at zero for the first
  fit test.
- A brim is normally unnecessary for the rear-down case. A 3–5 mm brim is
  optional for the separate keyboard if its front corners lift.

## Multicolour treatment

STL does not store filament colours. Put the case and keyboard on separate
plates/jobs to avoid unnecessary CFS colour swaps. Use surface painting in
Creality Print or OrcaSlicer:

- Case job: warm beige cabinet, near-black-green CRT, and bright-green prompt,
  cursor and status lens. With the case rear-down these are bounded final
  layers instead of colour changes through the full cabinet height.
- Keyboard job: warm beige deck, dark grey-brown normal keys/spacebar and a
  bright-green Enter key.
- Bay job: cabinet beige or a slightly darker beige, single colour.

All coloured faces are raised or bounded so surface-fill painting can select
them without extra colour STL files.

## Hidden watermark

All three print parts contain an enclosed `WDTY v0.5` void. The void is 0.60 mm
high at Z 0.60–1.20 mm: exactly three layers with the 0.20 mm K2 baseline. It
is invisible from outside and can be found in the slicer's layer preview.
Adaptive layer height changes the layer count, so keep the first 1.4 mm at
0.20 mm when preservation of the three-layer mark matters.

## CAD verification

- All three final STL files are closed, with zero nonmanifold edges.
- The keyboard is checked at its nominal installed position and has no hard
  collision with the cabinet or its four receiver pockets.
- The exact Gerber PCB and approximate component envelopes are sampled at 49
  positions through the PCB-to-bay insertion route.
- The complete loaded bay is sampled at 71 positions through the rear case
  opening. The shallow retention beads are intentionally excluded from the
  hard-collision check because they provide the designed friction contact.
- The routed PCB/component/cable envelopes do not intersect the rigid bay.
- Both lower guide lips overlap the PCB edges by 1 mm; support is checked
  by lowering the reference PCB through the nominal 0.30 mm clearance.
- The reset plunger has 0.30 mm nominal rest clearance to the approximate
  switch envelope and contacts it with 0.50 mm inward travel. This is a rigid
  envelope test, not a flexure or switch-force simulation.
- The floor latch is clear at the installed position and catches the PCB after
  0.70 mm movement toward the open end.
- The bay flange has 0.15 mm rest clearance and contacts the cabinet shoulder
  after 0.20 mm inward overtravel.
- The measured 4.3 mm cable envelope is checked at exactly 60 mm from the
  protruding USB-C edge to the outside of its relaxed U-bend.

## Mechanical audit / revised v0.5

The cabinet is now a hollow 2.0–2.4 mm shell. Thin longitudinal U-guides retain
the bay without a solid tunnel, and three 1.2 mm ribs support the fixed front
when it is printed rear-down. The front fascia overlaps the cabinet by 3.2 mm;
the bezel and CRT surface also have positive overlap, so the screen assembly
cannot become a detached or coplanar shell. Six nested screen profiles form a
shallow compound CRT bulge, while the bezel aperture widens toward the viewer
to create the avatar's inward-running surround. The glass centre remains just
behind the beige bezel face, protecting it during handling.

The original lower guide lips ended outside the PCB. They now extend inward
to match the upper lips, including the flared entrance. The cartridge also has
a four-layer structural front bridge below the underside component envelope,
an accessible PCB retention cantilever and open outside cable guides. The rear
trapezoidal opening is closed above the cartridge, and a two-stage body opening
plus shallow flange recess provides a defined insertion stop. Collision tests
use the complete case rather than only its core and fail on OpenSCAD errors.

Correction to the discussion: there were no duplicated PCB rails in the
case source; that diagnosis resulted from misreading a module boundary.

Unverified constraints: the component envelopes are approximations and the
PCB thickness is nominal. In particular, verify solder joints along the 1 mm
edge-contact strips, USB plug insertion depth, actual switch force/travel,
PLA latch force and cable stiffness before considering this mechanically
validated. The cable guides intentionally do not provide hard strain relief;
do not use either cable to pull the cartridge or PCB. These are prototype
limitations, not passed physical tests or a guaranteed fit.

Rebuild and check from the repository root:

```sh
openscad --export-format binstl -D 'part="case"' -o stl/v0.5/minitel_case_v0.5.stl src/minitel_v05.scad
openscad --export-format binstl -D 'part="keyboard"' -o stl/v0.5/minitel_keyboard_v0.5.stl src/minitel_v05.scad
openscad --export-format binstl -D 'part="bay"' -o stl/v0.5/minitel_esp32_bay_v0.5.stl src/minitel_v05.scad
python3 tools/check_v05_clearance.py
python3 tools/review_v05.py
```

Renders demonstrate the modelled route; the first physical print remains the
authority for filament shrinkage, cable stiffness and tactile-button travel.
